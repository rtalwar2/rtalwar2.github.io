Research

Two threads so far. The first is coding agents on real robots. Most of what a robot needs for a new task is already written down somewhere: a procedure, a few measured values, the checks that tell you it worked. I study what happens when a language model gets that text plus a coding interface to the robot, instead of a policy trained end to end: which parts transfer to new objects and task compositions, where it breaks, and what the failures say about perception, anchoring and body awareness. I do this with open-weight models on a single workstation, so the whole loop stays local, inspectable and cheap enough to log every trial.

The second is instrumentation for contact-rich manipulation: adding sensors to objects during training only, and using that privileged signal to shape representations a vision-and-audio policy can use at deployment. Button pressing is my testbed, with a microphone in the fingertip.

Before robotics I worked on explainable AI for machine-learning-based intrusion detection, and on autonomous drones for measurement tasks: RF-EMF exposure mapping and georeferencing buoys at sea.

Publications

Generalizing Manipulation Skills with a Local Coding Agent

Raman Talwar*, Elias Nijs*, Andreas Verleysen, Francis wyffels

Preprint, 2026 · IDLab-AIRO, Ghent University – imec · *equal contribution

A 27B open-weight model, served locally and given a coding harness, nine primitives and a hand-written procedure for one object, generalized that procedure to new colours, sizes, shapes and task compositions on a UR3e in 30 of 45 trials. We report every trial, the failure modes, and a second-pass effect that halves execution time.

You're Pushing My Buttons: Instrumented Learning of Gentle Button Presses

Raman Talwar, Remko Proesmans, Thomas Lips, Andreas Verleysen, Francis wyffels

Workshop on Learning and Representations for Active Perception in Manipulation, ICRA 2026, Vienna · arXiv:2604.05954

Can sensors that exist only at training time improve a manipulation policy without becoming a deployment dependency? We instrument a push button, use its state as privileged supervision to fine-tune an audio encoder on a fingertip microphone, and combine the result with diffusion-policy imitation learning. Success rates match the baselines, but the instrumentation-guided audio representations consistently press with less force.

Selected projects

The Finger 2024

Our new kitchen extractor hood has its buttons out of reach. Lowering it would break the fire-insurance rules and the official remote costs €150, so I built a small actuator that sits on the hood and presses the buttons for me, driven by a cheap infrared remote. An Arduino Nano Every is powered from the hood's own control board so the IR receiver is always listening. A transistor-switched 9 V battery powers the servo only for the moment it moves, so one battery lasts about six months. In daily use since 2024.

RF-EMF

Quadcopter-based autonomous monitoring of RF-EMF exposure Master's thesis, 2024–2025

A fully autonomous quadcopter with a broadband probe that maps radio-frequency electromagnetic field exposure and finds hotspots, replacing manual point measurements with repeatable, georeferenced flights. Most of the work was making the platform measure honestly: characterising the interference from the drone's own telemetry and radio link, the frequency-dependent shielding of the frame, and cleaning the signal accordingly. With the WAVES group at Ghent University – imec, supervised by Wout Joseph and Kenneth Deprez.

Brain Run Design project, 2023–2024

Temple Run on a real treadmill. Your running speed drives the character, hand movements steer it, and quiz questions generated from your own course material appear along the track. In a team of nine I built the hardware prototype and its interface to the game: a Hall sensor for belt speed and a lidar for incline, both calibrated.

XAI · IDS

Explainable AI for intrusion detection Master's thesis, 2022–2023

Intrusion-detection datasets often contain features that leak the label. I used SHAP to detect these contaminating features and inter-dataset generalization to measure how much they inflate reported performance.

Georeferencing buoys with drones Bachelor's thesis with Jan De Nul, 2022

Support vessels position buoys by hand for dredging operations. We built an Android app on the DJI Mobile SDK that flies a drone over the site, detects buoys in the video stream and georeferences them from the drone's sensors.

A cloud-shaped lamp on a Raspberry Pi whose colour follows the live weather of a chosen city. Clap twice to switch location. It also monitors indoor CO₂, humidity and temperature and flashes when the air gets bad. My first-year project.
